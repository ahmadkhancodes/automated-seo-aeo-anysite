# 06. Writing posts that bring clients

A post earns its place when a reader who is the right fit knows what to do next. Persuade with specifics, never with pressure.

## Structure
1. **The problem in the reader's terms.** Name what the status quo costs them, with a sourced number or an estimate you label as yours and show the assumption for.
2. **The solution, shown end to end.** What happens, step by step, what the reader's team does, where a person approves, what gets recorded.
3. **Proof you are allowed to use.** Real results, real cases, real credentials the owner provided. If there are none, say what you can show instead (a method, a sample, a test) and never invent a testimonial.
4. **"What would a 30-day pilot deliver?"** (or "How to get started"): concrete deliverables by week, what the client provides, how success is measured. This is the section that tells a reader what contacting you gets them. The gate checks that it exists.
5. **"Is this right for you?"** Three to five honest signs that the reader is a fit, and one line on who should not buy. Readers trust a page that tells some of them to stop.
6. **One closing line** that recommends a specific next step for this specific offer and says why now ("plan next year's budget around it", "start the pilot in the first quarter"). Name the offer; do not write generic "contact us" text.

## Links and calls to action
- Link in the body to the matching conversion page (service, product, booking) with descriptive anchor text. Set `conversion.requireInternalPrefix` so the gate enforces it.
- If the site template already adds a call-to-action block, do not duplicate it inside the Markdown. If it does not, add one short, plain call to action after the closing line.

## What never to write
- Fake urgency or scarcity ("limited spots", "act now", "only 3 left"). The gate blocks it.
- Unsourced superlatives ("the best", "guaranteed results").
- Invented numbers, clients, reviews or quotes.
- Disparaging a competitor without a sourced, fair comparison.

## Adapting by business type
- **Services:** the pilot section describes a first engagement; the closing line recommends a scoped estimate.
- **SaaS:** replace the pilot with a trial plan ("what to set up in week one") and link to the trial page.
- **E-commerce:** replace it with a buyer's guide step ("how to choose, what to check") and link to the relevant collection.
- **Affiliate or media:** disclose the relationship clearly, compare options fairly, and link to the offer with the disclosure near the link.
